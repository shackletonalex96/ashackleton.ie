In-House Prototyping

2019 – 2026

A recurring theme across my career — building and expanding in-house prototyping capabilities from scratch.

Main Roles

Prototyping Lead

At every company I have worked at, I have taken the initiative to establish or significantly expand in-house mechanical prototyping capabilities. This is a thread that connects my time at Trinity College Dublin, Firecomms, and SumUp.

What I did

  • Facility Expansion.
  • Equipment Procurement.
  • Maintenance & Operations.
  • Business Case Development.

Trinity College Dublin

2019 – Additive Manufacturing Centre.

At Trinity I was in charge of the student-led additive manufacturing centre, responsible for the training, maintenance, and day-to-day operation of all 3D printers in the college. I led a project to expand the facility, building the business case, pitching to senior management, procuring new equipment, and overseeing the installation — ultimately doubling the capacity of the centre.

Firecomms

2021–2023 — First in-house mechanical prototyping.

At Firecomms I pushed for the company's first in-house mechanical prototyping equipment, reducing prototype lead times and costs by 90%. The prototypes produced on this equipment were integral to the development of several of the bespoke optoelectronic connectors I designed during my time there. Before this initiative, all mechanical prototyping was outsourced — a slow and expensive process that was a significant bottleneck in the design cycle.

SumUp

2024–2026 — Reviving and expanding prototyping infrastructure.

When I joined SumUp, the existing 3D printers and prototyping equipment were in a state of disrepair. I took charge of the maintenance and upkeep, performing essential repairs to bring all equipment back to a fully functional state and allowing the company to begin in-house prototyping again. I then led efforts to upgrade the outdated equipment by pitching business cases to upper management, demonstrating over €20k in savings on mechanical prototypes alone and a 90% reduction in lead times. I also led the expansion of the physical space available for prototyping, giving the engineering team a dedicated area to iterate quickly on mechanical designs.
